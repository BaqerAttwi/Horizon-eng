§01

Roles & access

Five roles, each seeing a different slice of the same data. Permissions are baked into the login token, so a role change only takes effect after the person logs in again.

Owner
  • Sees everything, including cost & profit
  • Only role that can approve projects (admin approval)
  • Only role that sets a project's payment deadline
  • Assigns technicians to projects
Accounting
  • Records payments, views Debt page
  • Sees pricing, not restricted like Engineer
  • Cannot approve projects
Engineer
  • Only sees projects they own or collaborate on
  • Cost column hidden everywhere
  • Price edits on their own projects need owner approval first
Secretary
  • Products, clients, reservations, announcements
  • No project pricing access
Technician
  • Only the projects an owner assigned them to
  • Execution checklist only — no price, no cost, ever
  • Marks panels/items complete in the field
Worth knowing

A technician can never see a dollar figure anywhere in the app — not the item price, not the panel total, not the project value. Their entire view is quantities and completion checkboxes.

§02

Projects & CRM

A project is a tree: Panels → Divisions → Items. Divisions are fixed categories — Incoming, Outgoing, Enclosure, Accessories, Measurement — so every panel is built the same way.

Building a project

  • Create manually, or import a PDF quote — the parser reads panel/division/item structure straight from the document and creates the whole tree in one step
  • Panel count set at creation auto-generates that many blank panels on first open
  • Adding or removing panels afterward keeps the panel count and progress percentage in sync automatically
  • Copy Panel pulls a fully-priced panel from any other project you have access to — useful for repeat layouts

Item Groups vs manual items

An item can come from the product catalog, be typed in manually, or be dropped in as a pre-built Item Group — a reusable bundle of parts (see §11) that expands into real priced items the moment it's added to a division.

§03

Pricing formula

Every item runs through the same five-step calculation, in this order. Discount comes before markup, manpower is calculated on the post-discount base, not the final price.

1. after_discount = base_price × qty × (1 − discount%)
2. markup_amt    = after_discount × markupP%
3. manpower_amt  = after_discount × manpower%
4. markupM_amt   = manpower_amt × markupM%
5. final_price     = after_discount + markup_amt + manpower_amt + markupM_amt

Panel-level markup/manpower percentages cascade down to every division and item under it — unless an item's price has been edited by hand, which flags it as override and exempts it from future panel-level cascades.

Currency

Every price is stored in both EUR and USD. The conversion rate defaults to 1 EUR = 1.18 USD on a new project, and is editable per project at any time — changing it recalculates every item's USD figure using its stored EUR base price.

Live preview

While editing an item, every figure on that row — after-discount, markup, final price, profit — recalculates as you type, before you hit Save. Nothing is confirmed until Save is clicked; Cancel discards the whole edit.

§04

Approvals

Two independent approval flags gate a project: admin approval (owner only) and client approval. Both must be approved before a project counts in revenue, profit, or debt tracking anywhere in the app.

Price change requests

An engineer editing the base price on their own project doesn't change it directly — it files a price change request instead, sitting as pending until an owner approves or rejects it. Everyone else's price edits (markup, discount, manpower, cost) apply immediately without approval.

§05

Execution & technicians

Once a project is client-approved, an Execution tab appears — a price-free checklist of every panel and item, for tracking physical installation progress.

  • Owners assign technicians to specific projects; a technician only ever sees their assigned work under My Projects
  • Marking a panel complete cascades to every item inside it automatically — and reverses cleanly if unchecked
  • Items can be partially completed by quantity (e.g. 6 of 10 installed), not just all-or-nothing
§06

Payments & debt

A project's total is rarely paid in one transfer. Every payment is logged individually, and each one can set the deadline for the next one.

How the deadline works

  1. First payment — no deadline needed yet
  2. Recording the second payment, you're prompted for when the remaining balance is expected
  3. That date becomes the project's payment deadline until the next payment updates it — or the balance clears, which removes it automatically

The Debt page

Owner Accounting — a dedicated page (and a tab inside Analytics) listing every approved project still owing money, sorted by whichever deadline is most overdue. Click a project to see its full payment history inline, no page change.

Automatic reminders

A background check flags any approved project past its payment deadline — or, if none was ever set, 30 days of inactivity — and notifies the owner and the project's engineer without anyone having to remember to check.

§07

Analytics

Revenue and profit are counted on a cash basis — money actually received — not the full contract value the moment a project is approved.

FigureHow it's calculated
RevenueSUM of payments actually recorded
Recognized costtotal_cost × (paid ÷ total_due)
Profitrevenue − recognized cost

Cost is recognized proportionally as payments come in, so a project that's 40% paid shows 40% of its eventual profit — and lands on the exact full figure once fully paid. The monthly revenue chart is grouped by payment date, with a breakdown of which project each month's money came from.

§08

Notifications

A background check runs periodically and covers four things, each notifying the relevant people without manual triggering:

  • Deadlines — project due within 3 days
  • Approvals — stuck pending for 24h+ (admin) or 48h+ (client)
  • Stock — product at or below reserved quantity
  • Payments — see §06
§09

Files & links

Rather than uploading files to the server, the Files tab stores links — paste a share link from wherever the file actually lives, give it a name, optionally attach it to a specific panel. One link field also lives at the project level for a general "all files" folder.

§10

Import / export

  • PDF import — parses a quote PDF into a full project structure, previewed before creation
  • Excel import — bulk product catalog updates, up to ~11,000 rows
  • CSV/Excel export — products, projects, reservations, per-project CRM detail, and full analytics
§11

Item groups

A named, reusable bundle of parts with default quantities and pricing — build it once, drop it into any division on any project. Dropping one in expands it into real, individually-editable items; the group template itself is untouched.

§12

Sessions & security

  • Login sessions last ~12 hours; expiry shows a clear "please log in again" message, not a silent failure
  • A deleted worker's session stops working on their very next request, even mid-session
  • Repeated failed logins are rate-limited
  • Every project-scoped page checks that the logged-in engineer actually owns or collaborates on that project — a stray link can't be used to peek at someone else's work
